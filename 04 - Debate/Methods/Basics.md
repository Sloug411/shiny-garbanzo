What is your plan? | Argue to maintain the<br>status quo. Why is the<br>current law or policy sufficient? Why is a change unnecessary? | Argue against the resolved. Affirmative Constructive Speakers
● **The first constructive speaker is required to state a plan. Remember that the resolved is
not the plan.**
● You may use a portion of your time to refute contentions brought up by the negative
team so far in the debate.

Negative Constructive Speakers
● **Show that all the contentions of the affirmative team are not valid – either that the
problems as described by the affirmative do not exist, the affirmative proposals do not
solve any existing problems, or both.**
● Refute contentions brought up by the affirmative team.

All Constructive Speakers
● Visual aids are permissible. Once you introduce a visual aid, it becomes available for the
opponent’s use if desired. Visual aids should be left in plain view of the opposition.
● After each constructive speech, a member of the opposing team will cross examine you.
During this time, the cross examiner will attempt to destroy the contentions of your
speech.
● You can earn up to 7.5 points for your team (5 points for the constructive speech and 2.5
points for your responses to the cross examiner’s questions).
